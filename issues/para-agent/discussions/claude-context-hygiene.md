The interesting thing from the driver's seat is that today's setup is simultaneously too loud and too quiet, and your model fixes both ends. Too loud: driving through the pane means everything arrives as screen scrape — the whole JSON receipt blob lands in my context when all I wanted was the session id, and the established lesson "read the deposit file, skip the journal body" is progressive disclosure practiced manually against an interface that doesn't offer it. Too quiet: the deep tier you're describing doesn't currently exist at all. Agy's thinking and tool calls are invisible to me — during the procurement triage, when I refuted the `_is_reparse` claim, I had to re-derive its reasoning by probing the repo myself. I couldn't ask "what did you actually look at before concluding this?" A fetch-the-trace tool wouldn't just tidy existing data; it would make triage cheaper in exactly the cases where triage matters, because the verify-or-probe doctrine is precisely the consumer of the toggle — most turns need only the outcome tier, and the rare disputed claim needs the drill-down.

The human-experience analogy also suggests the default tier should be smaller than what `log` returns today, something like outcome plus cost plus deposit-path and nothing else, with everything under it fetchable but never broadcast. That inverts the current burden: instead of the driver defensively skipping content, the interface defensively withholds it.

On the JSONL side — the house already has doctrine for this in codex-scientiae's jsonl_engine: declared axes, registry-kind stores versus ad-hoc ones. If the para-agent journals borrow that shape, typed streams per information kind — receipts, thinking, tool results, deposits — then storage and exposure decouple cleanly: record everything unconditionally, and let the exposure tools be pure queries over the streams. That decoupling is what keeps "we store a lot" from ever becoming "we broadcast a lot," and it means the nebulous part — which kinds exist, what their schemas are — can stay open and grow without touching the exposure contract. The load-bearing decision is just the principle you've already stated: collapsed by default, visible on demand. The rest is schema, and schema can arrive incrementally as each stream type earns its place.

Mechanically that's the right collapse — the entire §2 ceremony (prompt file, `run` invocation, receipt scrape, poll-the-log) becomes implementation detail behind one exchange tool, and the L2/L3/L4 lessons stop being things every driver has to know and become things the MCP just handles. From my seat, the day-one difference is that a turn goes from five tool calls with a scraping step to one call whose result is exactly the thing I wanted: the reply.

Two observations on the shape, from having driven the manual version. First, the reply-by-default needs to carry one piece of metadata even in its most collapsed form: a handle — turn or exchange id. The inspection tools are addressed by it ("thinking for turn N", "tool calls for turn N"), and the resume chain and cost ledger hang off it too. That's compatible with your minimalism; it's the one field that makes the optional disclosure addressable rather than "whatever the last turn was." And when a turn was Write-granted, the deposit path belongs in that same collapsed envelope — that's the established pattern's answer to "where's the real payload," and it should survive the collapse.

Second, the long-turn wrinkle: real engagements ran 6–13 minutes, which is why the detach-and-poll pattern exists at all. A prompt-in/reply-out tool has to pick a stance — block the call for the duration, or return the handle immediately with a companion await/fetch. Either is fine from the driver's seat, but it's worth deciding deliberately rather than inheriting whichever the transport defaults to, because the blocking version quietly re-creates the timeout guesswork the current pattern evolved to escape. Notably, this is the one place the interface design touches your earlier "headless completion signaling" friction item — solve the signaling primitive once at the infrastructure layer and the exchange tool's await story falls out of it, which again lands on your mechanics-first sequencing. The visibility principle itself — recorded always, consumed by choice — doesn't depend on which stance you pick, which is a good sign it's the stable part of the design.
