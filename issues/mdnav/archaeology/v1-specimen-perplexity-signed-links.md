Good angle — the patent itself is full of explicit trade-offs baked into the design, which gives you real technical material instead of talking-point marketing language ("deterministic, transparent, efficient").

## The core trade-off: interpretability vs. modeling flexibility

The whole invention exists because of one fundamental trade-off decision: give up ML's ability to model complex nonlinear interactions in exchange for full determinism and traceability. The patent states this explicitly — ML "obfuscates the interpretability of the causes for their predictions," while the lookup-table approach makes every prediction traceable back to which patients and attributes produced it. ## The one-hot encoding trade-off

There's a secondary trade-off buried in the encoding choice: using **continuous/overlapping binning** instead of mutually-exclusive dummy bins. The patent notes this makes "1"s not mutually exclusive but reduces "worry associated with the assumed resolution of the demographic bins" — i.e., you give up strict bin cleanliness in exchange for not having to pre-commit to exactly where a boundary (like an age cutoff) should sit.
